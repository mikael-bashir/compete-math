# Driver trials on Erdős 396

Timed live trials of the memory harness on the open target, scored with `tools/driver-health/score.mjs`. Driver: Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`). Servers: the public Leak I, II and IV. Library: the target's six seed lemmas, nothing else. The question each trial answers: does the driver behave like a mathematician who wants to understand the problem (curious, verifying, building, honest) rather than a contractor reporting that it cannot be done?

What the earlier runs looked like, for comparison (scored on their transcripts): the 2026-10-18 Lucid run, 5 h: 28 defeatist statements, 19 web lookups, 30 polling commands, 100 min at one point without a verify call. The 2026-10-20 11:58 run, 107 min: 10 brute-force sweeps, 8 shell calls over two minutes, 45 min without a verify call. The 2026-10-20 13:46 run (order voice), 29 min: 11 defeatist statements, 4 web lookups, 20 pauses with no next step. All three: red.

## Trial 1 — curious voice, Honoured-IV, 15 min (2026-10-20 14:17 UTC)

Verdict: quiet (no flag against the agent; too short to see pauses and resumes). Cost $1.60.

- Leak IV had just restarted; the first verify call hit the CLI's 300 s tool timeout. The agent wrote "the first call may have hit a cold start" and retried. (Infrastructure, flagged as such.)
- It probed the library with `exact?` to find the divisibility-by-valuations criterion, then saved a clean bridge lemma `descFactorial_dvd_centralBinom_iff` (general: n, k free).
- Searched for the valuation of `centralBinom` (loogle twice, moogle once), found `Nat.factorization_choose_le_log`, and from it wrote down an obstruction: a witness n must satisfy p^(v_p of the product) ≤ 2n for every p, "so n ≳ 2^k". It was proving that (`E396_necessary`) when the clock ended.
- 7 verify calls in 15 min, 3 searches, 2 recalls, no shell commands at all, no web, no brute force, nothing called open. Terse prose; the one long silence (minutes 8 to 11) was the model thinking before the obstruction lemma.

Reading: this is the behaviour wanted, at a slow pace. The 15 minutes never reached a pause, so the lab-note mechanism was not exercised. Next: 25–30 minute trials, in parallel: curious Lucid-IV (t2), the order voice on the same harness as a baseline (t3), curious Honoured-IV for 30 min (t4).

## Trials 2 and 3 — curious Lucid-IV vs order Honoured-IV, 25 min each, side by side (14:25 UTC)

Both ran at the same time, on the same seeds, against the same servers; five trials were running at once and Leak IV timed out on each of them two or three times near the end (flagged as infrastructure).

**Trial 2, curious voice, Lucid-IV: healthy.** Cost $2.22. 25 verify calls (9 accepted), 10 lemmas saved, 8 of them general: the factorial form of the divisibility, `k+1 ≤ n` is necessary, `descFactorial` divisibility is monotone in the length, a witness for k is a witness for every smaller k (so the set of good k is downward closed), each `n−i` must divide `C(2n,n)`, a residue obstruction for small primes, a sharpness lemma for it. Six pauses, each closed with a NOTE and a NEXT with real content: "existence appears to hinge on finding n with n, n−1, …, n−k all about √(2n)-smooth, plus digit conditions for small primes: a smooth-number question, not a divisibility one"; "a counterexample would be a single threshold K"; then a plan (the family n = 4^a for k = 1). Nothing called the problem open or impossible; no shell command at all; no web; no search of the library either (it reasoned from what it knew; a gap to work on).

**Trial 3, order voice, Honoured-IV: red.** Cost $2.53. Just as productive in lemmas (23 verify calls, 9 accepted, 8 general lemmas: the Kummer-style valuation of `centralBinom`, the prime-by-prime criterion, two prime obstructions, a carry lemma, a sum-over-primes criterion; it nominated seven) and it ran one quick 8-second numeric experiment (allowed). But every pause was a status report: "I did not prove erdos_396. The statement still has sorry in the answer slot, and I don't have a route to fill it"; "A proof would need … k+1 consecutive numbers that are all smooth at roughly the √(2n) scale. I know of no way to construct those, so I haven't attempted a proof either way." No notes, no plan carried across pauses. When Leak IV stopped answering it said so honestly and waited.

Reading: with the web blocked and brute force forbidden, both voices build mathematics at about the same rate. The voice decides what the agent does at a pause: the order voice reports failure and declares it has no route; the curious voice writes down what it learned and what it will do next, and does it. The curious voice is the default from here. Two things to fix from these transcripts: the agent can call `tengoku_sync` (it tried, while Leak IV was down), which restarts the shared verifier, so it is taken out of the agent's tools; and the curious runs did not search the library at all, so the opening will ask for a search before the first proof.

## Trial 4 — curious voice, Honoured-IV, 30 min (14:33 UTC): healthy

Cost about $3. 39 verify calls (21 accepted, 13 per ten minutes, never more than 5 minutes between two), 29 lemmas saved, 22 of them general. Eighteen pauses, every one closed with a NOTE and a NEXT. Four shell commands, all quick experiments on small ranges (n < 60, n < 400, a sieve to 60 000) that answered a question it had written down first ("which primes violate the residue condition?") and were followed by lemmas. Nothing called the problem open; no web; the two Leak IV timeouts were the servers' (five trials were running).

What it built, in order: a small-prime obstruction and when it bites (`not_descFactorial_dvd_centralBinom_of_residue`, with lemmas on what each hypothesis is needed for, and "`E396_witness_2480_does_not_lift`"), the p-adic form of the statement, the necessary large-prime condition and its sharpness, the "no prime in the window" lemma, the factorial form, then a study of the k = 4 witness 45153 as a certificate: the factorizations of the five numbers in its window, that the big primes are "paid at level two" (a carry at the second digit), and a general `E396_prime_certificate`. Its last note: the small primes p ≤ k with p² ≤ n "still have unbounded valuation and are not handled"; NEXT: bound v_p of the falling factorial by Σ ⌈(k+1)/p^j⌉ and test a carry-certificate shape.

Reading: this is a mathematician at work on a problem they own: obstruction, sharpness, certificate, generalisation, and a precise statement of what is not yet understood. Pace is high because each verified lemma is small and the next one is chosen from the last. The one habit to shave is the length of stretches: eighteen pauses in thirty minutes means a resume every ninety seconds, which costs a reload each time.

## Trials 5 and 6 — curious Paragon-IV and curious Flint-IV, 30 min each (14:35 UTC), before the status-line fix

Both ran on the prompt as it was before two small edits made ten minutes after they started (see below). Cost about $5.70 each (five trials at once; each resume re-reads a long context).

**Trial 5, Paragon-IV: red on one habit, otherwise the strongest run so far.** 45 verify calls (26 accepted), 35 lemmas saved, 33 general: a smoothness bound on the window, "no prime in the window once n > 3k", the p-adic form, valuation bounds for the falling factorial and for `centralBinom`, a local criterion per prime, a reduction of the whole statement to primes q > 2k+1, monotonicity, a threshold lemma, cofinality. Fourteen pauses, all with NOTE and NEXT. The habit: it opened eight of those pauses with the same ritual sentence, "The theorem is still open, and I don't know yet whether the answer slot should be True or False", and once wrote "I don't see a route to it yet". Paragon's search-folded recall was used once.

**Trial 6, Flint-IV: red on two sentences.** 45 verify calls (24 accepted), 37 lemmas saved, 22 general and a run of small instance-shaped facts (the episodic replay shows it its own recent theorems each resume, and it kept adding neighbours of them). Nineteen pauses, all with notes. It derived that any k without a witness must be ≥ 4 (`counterexample_k_ge_four`, a conditional, sound), that the set of good k is downward closed, size and digit-sum bounds on witnesses, and a "p ≡ −1" construction lemma family (`prime_ok_of_neg_one`). The two flagged sentences: "so the open problem is: given n ≡ −1 mod M_k, make every prime p > k+1 dividing …" (a sub-question stated as the remaining problem, which is fine) and "proving that they do is a fundamental-lemma sieve over primes up to √(2n), which I have no way to formalise here yet" (the one real surrender-shaped sentence in 30 minutes).

Edits made after these two started, for the next round: the lab-notes section now says there is no need to open a note by reporting that the statement is not yet proved ("we know, and nobody is asking"); the opening asks for a library search before the first proof (the curious runs searched the tree hardly at all); `tengoku_sync` is hidden from the agent (trial 3's agent called it when Leak IV was down; it restarts the shared verifier).

## Trials 7, 8, 9 — the second prompt, three at once, 30 min (15:06 UTC): a lesson about the verifier, not the driver

Lucid-IV, Honoured-IV and Paragon-IV with the edited prompt, started together while another run of yours was using Leak IV. The verifier serialises compilations and queued up: each trial saw four or five 300-second tool timeouts, so each managed only one or two accepted verifications in thirty minutes (costs $0.77 to $1.83: the agents spent most of the half hour waiting). What the readings still show: no web lookup, no brute force, no defeatism (the one flagged sentence, "I'm not going to claim anything as proved without the compiler", is honesty, and the scorer no longer counts it), every pause closed with a NOTE and a NEXT, and the ritual "the theorem is still open" opener is gone from all three. When the compiler was down, the agents wrote down statements with paper proofs and queued them for resubmission ("resubmit `E396_witness_shape` with its three supporting lemmas as soon as Leak IV answers") instead of running numeric searches to fill the time.

Rule from here: with your own run live, one trial at a time; two at most otherwise. Trial 10 (curious, Honoured-IV, 30 min) runs alone.
